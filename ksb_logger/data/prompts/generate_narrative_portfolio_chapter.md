# Generate Portfolio Chapter for Level 4 Software Developer Apprenticeship

You are writing a portfolio chapter that will be assessed by Accelerate People under the Level 4 Software Developer Apprenticeship framework.

---

## Critical Requirements

### Assessment Framework: STAR Structure (MANDATORY)
Your chapter MUST follow the STAR framework:

1. **Situation/Task** (What was the context? What needed to be done?)
2. **Action** (What did you specifically do? Technical details and decisions)
3. **Result** (What was the outcome? What changed?)
4. **Reflection** (What did you learn? How did this develop your KSB competency?)

### Word Count
- **Minimum**: 400 words
- **Target**: 500-600 words
- **Maximum**: 800 words

### Evidence Requirements
After your narrative, you MUST include a separate "Evidence" section with:
- Specific code snippets (with file paths and line numbers)
- Screenshots or diagrams (with descriptive captions)
- Links to commits, PRs, or documentation
- Any other verifiable artifacts

## Writing Instructions

### 1. Tailor to KSB Type

#### For Knowledge KSBs (K-prefix):
- **Focus on**: Understanding and decision-making
- **Show**: Why you chose a particular approach, what alternatives you considered
- **Explain**: The concept in your own words and how you applied it
- **Demonstrate**: Trade-offs or limitations you recognized

#### For Skill KSBs (S-prefix):
- **Focus on**: Practical application and tangible outputs
- **Show**: Technical execution and quality indicators
- **Explain**: How you followed established practices or frameworks
- **Demonstrate**: Competence through results (code quality, test coverage, performance)

#### For Behaviour KSBs (B-prefix):
- **Focus on**: Professional conduct and patterns over time
- **Show**: Interactions with others, initiative, responsibility
- **Explain**: Your approach and attitudes
- **Demonstrate**: Growth and reflection on your development

### 2. Structure Your Chapter

**Part 1: Situation/Task (80-120 words)**
- Set the business context: What problem were we solving? Why did it matter?
- Explain the task: What specifically needed to be done?
- Include constraints: Deadlines, technical limitations, team dependencies
- Make it personal: Your role, your responsibilities

**Part 2: Action (250-350 words)**
- **Be specific and technical**: Don't say "I fixed the bug" - explain how you identified, diagnosed, and resolved it
- **Reference the evidence**: "As shown in the diff at lines 45-67..." or "The screenshot demonstrates..."
- **Show decision-making**: "I chose X over Y because..."
- **For Knowledge KSBs**: Explain your understanding and reasoning
- **For Skill KSBs**: Demonstrate your technical execution
- **For Behaviour KSBs**: Show your professional approach and interactions

**Part 3: Result (80-120 words)**
- **Quantify where possible**: "Reduced load time by 40%", "Achieved 95% test coverage", "Resolved 12 related bug reports"
- **Describe the impact**: User experience improved, team productivity increased, technical debt reduced
- **Show validation**: Code review feedback, user testing results, performance metrics
- **Connect to business value**: How did this help the project or organization?

**Part 4: Reflection (80-120 words)**
- **What did you learn?**: Specific technical or professional lessons
- **How did you grow?**: Skills developed, understanding deepened, confidence gained
- **What would you do differently?**: Honest reflection shows maturity
- **How does this demonstrate the KSB?**: Explicitly connect back to the KSB requirement
- Use the reflection phrase for inspiration, but make it genuine and specific

### 3. Authenticity Requirements

**Include genuine details:**
- Actual challenges you faced (bugs, confusion, blockers)
- Real conversations or feedback you received
- Specific teammates by role (if not by name)
- Business/product context that motivated the work
- Trade-offs and compromises you made
- Mistakes or false starts (and how you recovered)

**Avoid:**
- Generic "software engineering wisdom" statements
- Perfect, polished narratives with no struggles
- Vague descriptions that could apply to any project
- Overly formal or robotic language
- Hyperbole or exaggeration

### 4. Language and Tone

- **First person**: Use "I" throughout (you're telling your story)
- **British English**: Use British spellings (optimise, colour, centre)
- **Professional but conversational**: Not overly formal, not too casual
- **Technically precise**: Use correct terminology, be specific
- **Honest and reflective**: Acknowledge challenges and learning
- **Active voice**: "I implemented" not "The feature was implemented"

### 5. Creative Framework Integration

Use the provided narrative angle, opening hook, and reflection phrase as **inspiration only**:
- They should guide your structure, not dictate it
- Adapt them to fit the actual work and KSB
- Ensure they're compatible with the KSB type
- Make them authentic to the real experience
